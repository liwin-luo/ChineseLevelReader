Your phone has four hundred photos of your dog and exactly zero framed pictures of him dressed as a knight. But the difference between a portrait that's frameable and one that looks nothing like your pet comes down to a few decisions you make before you ever click "generate." Here's how these tools work, which photos to use, and what to ask for.

How to get a portrait that actually looks like your pet

This is the part that separates good results from "whose cat is this?" Input quality beats everything else.

Start with one clear, front-facing photo. Face fully visible, eyes open, good light. This is the anchor the AI builds the likeness from.

Add one or two more angles if you have them. A side profile and a seated shot help the AI keep the face and fur consistent across the image — that's why the upload allows up to four photos.

Keep it solo. One pet per portrait, no humans in the frame. The tools are built for pets only, and group shots or blurry phone photos are the most common reason a generation comes back mediocre. If a result misses, don't pay for a retry — regenerating is free, and a sharper photo usually fixes it.

Mind the file basics. JPG, PNG, or WebP, up to 5 MB each. A crisp recent photo beats a scanned photo from 2010.

What to ask for: eight scene ideas that always work

You can describe any pet-related scene — setting, outfit, art style, mood — or start from ready-made templates. Popular ones to try:

  1. Royal classic — the Renaissance oil painting with a gold frame. This is the genre that made pet portraits a thing; it suits solemn pets best.
  2. Sticker — your pet with a thick white border, ready for a laptop or water bottle.
  3. Movie poster — your dog as the lead in a film that doesn't exist. Great for breeds with "main character" energy.
  4. Astronaut — a helmet reflecting the stars. Works for nearly every pet, including the unphotographable ones.
  5. Animated film — big-eyed 3D-cartoon version of your pet. The style kids ask for.
  6. Polaroid — a flash-lit instant photo look, casually staged.
  7. Neon night — your pet under magenta-and-cyan city lights, for the moody-shot aesthetic.
  8. Christmas card — fireplace bokeh and seasonal warmth. Generate in October, mail in December.

Print it, gift it, share it
